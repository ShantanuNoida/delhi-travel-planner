# Itinerary Quality Review & Recommendations

*A skeptical, domain-expert review of the actual travel plans this app produces — judged the way an experienced Delhi travel agent or seasoned local would judge them. This is a different review from `QA-UX-Review-and-Recommendations.md`, which covered crashes, UI polish, and rate-limit handling. Here the question is only: **are the itineraries any good?***

---

## 1. Summary

**Method.** I drove the real backend (`phase3/agent.TravelAgent` → `phase2/poi_search` + `phase2/itinerary_builder` → `phase3/itinerary_narrator`) directly in Python, with a fresh `TravelAgent` per scenario, across **10 genuinely diverse briefs**: single narrow interests (just food; just photography; just nightlife; just art; just religion), broad combos (food+history; culture+shopping+nature; history+architecture; food+history+nature), day counts 1–4, group sizes 1–4, paces relaxed/moderate/intensive, and **2 scenarios with follow-up EDIT commands**. Every scenario's itinerary JSON, spoken summary, and narrative were saved under `scratchpad/itinerary-quality-review/scenario_*.json`; every claim below quotes those files. I also traced the ranking/scheduling pipeline against the raw dataset (`phase1/data/pois.json`, 5,078 POIs) to root-cause each issue.

**Headline finding.** The itineraries are **geographically plausible but touristically hollow**. The single most important problem: **Delhi's iconic, must-see monuments are systematically excluded.** Across all 10 itineraries, **Red Fort, Qutub Minar, Humayun's Tomb, India Gate, and Purana Qila appear a combined ZERO times** — even though all five exist in the dataset, correctly categorised as `monument`, with correct visit durations (Red Fort 180 min, Qutub 120 min, Humayun 120 min). A "4-day history & architecture" trip (Scenario 4) contains none of them. No competent Delhi planner would produce that. This is not a data gap — it is a ranking bug, root-caused in §3 R-1.

**What's genuinely good** (to keep): the geographic clustering does keep most consecutive stops in the same part of the city; the ≤45-min single-leg cap holds; real KB entry fees (e.g. Jama Masjid photography charge) and "verify locally" hedging flow through honestly; grounding auto-correction and feasibility caveats work as designed. The problems below are about *selection and shaping*, not fabrication.

**Limitations.** Chat/reasoning runs on Gemini `gemini-flash-lite-latest` (shared free tier, ~15 req/min); I paced the 10 builds to stay under the cap and hit no blocking quota failures. One EDIT (Scenario 10) surfaced a Unicode console crash that is likely specific to the Windows CLI harness (see F-10) — I flag it but treat the *substance* of that edit, not the crash, as the finding.

**Counts:** **5 High**, **4 Medium**, **3 Low** priority recommendations.

## 2. Findings (grouped by theme)

### F-1 — Iconic must-see sights are systematically absent *(the big one)*

Not one of the 10 itineraries includes Red Fort, Qutub Minar, Humayun's Tomb, India Gate, or Purana Qila. Concrete cases:

- **Scenario 4 — "4-day history & architecture, moderate, 2 people."** The four days are built around: Jama Masjid (×3 across the trip — see F-3), Shanker's Doll Museum, Hazrat Nizamuddin Dargah, Archaeological Museum, Hanuman Mandir (×2), several obscure churches (LDS, St James', St Mary's, St Thomas Orthodox), Dilli Haat, and Lotus Temple once. **No Red Fort, no Qutub Minar, no Humayun's Tomb.** For the single most history-focused brief in the set, this is indefensible — these are the three UNESCO World Heritage sites that *define* a Delhi history trip.
- **Scenario 1 — "food & history."** Day 1 is led by *Shanker's International Doll Museum*; Day 2 is Jama Masjid + Chandni Chowk + two war/partition museums. Chandni Chowk and Jama Masjid sit **right beside Red Fort**, yet Red Fort is never scheduled — a local would never walk a tourist past the Red Fort to a doll museum instead.

**Root cause (traced, quantified).** In `poi_search.py::_relevance_score`, an iconic monument scores `0.6 (category) + 0.3 (HIGH_PROFILE) = 0.90`, but gets **+0.1 only if it happens to carry an OSM `opening_hours` tag**. Red Fort, Qutub Minar, Humayun's Tomb, India Gate, and Purana Qila all have `opening_hours = "unknown"` in the data, so they stall at **0.90**. Meanwhile Dilli Haat, Lotus Temple, and Jama Masjid *do* carry an hours tag and reach **1.00** — purely an OSM data-completeness artefact, unrelated to importance. Then `poi_search` takes the top 20 via **round-robin across matched categories**. Adding interests widens the category set, shrinking each category's share:

| Brief | Matched categories | Monument slots in top-20 | Icons that survive |
|---|---|---|---|
| `history` alone | 2 (monument, museum) | 10 | Red Fort, Humayun's only (Qutub/India Gate/Purana still cut) |
| `food` + `history` | 4 | 5 | **NONE** |
| `history` + `architecture` | 5 (adds temple/mosque/church) | ~4 | **NONE** |

So a single-interest history trip barely surfaces 2 of 5 icons, and **any multi-interest trip — the common case — surfaces zero**, because the tag-lucky non-icons (Dilli Haat, Lotus Temple, Jama Masjid) monopolise the handful of monument slots and the real icons sit at monument-bucket ranks 7–12, below the cut. Even in the `history`-alone case where Red Fort reaches the top-20, the zone-clustering + budget scheduler can still drop it (its correct 180-min duration makes it hard to fit — see F-5). **Two failure layers stacked.**

### F-2 — Interest extraction silently distorts or drops the user's stated interest

The LLM extraction step (`agent.py::_extract_context` / `EXTRACT_SYSTEM`) normalises niche interests into generic buckets *before* they ever reach `INTEREST_MAP` — which actually has good mappings for them:

- **Scenario 7 — asked for "religion and spirituality."** Extracted `interests = ["culture", "history"]`. `INTEREST_MAP` has `religion → temple/mosque/church/gurdwara` and `spirituality → …` ready to use, but they were discarded. Result: the "spiritual circuit" is padded with the Doll Museum, Indian War Memorial Museum, and Chandni Chowk — a history trip wearing a religion label.
- **Scenario 8 — asked for "art."** Extracted `interests = ["culture"]`. `art → museum, park` exists but was dropped for the catch-all `culture`, so Day 3 is Akshardham + Gurudwara Jharsa + Hanuman Mandir — **three religious sites back-to-back on an "art" trip**, zero galleries.

The chat reply *acknowledges* the interest, but the built plan honours a different one. That's a constraint-honouring failure the user can't see coming.

### F-3 — The same real place is scheduled repeatedly under different categories

`itinerary_builder.py::_duplicate_of` only dedups within the **same category** on the **same day**. The dataset stores single real places under multiple categories and multiple OSM records, so duplicates leak through:

- **Scenario 4:** *Jama Masjid* is scheduled **three times** in one 4-day trip — as `mosque` (Day 1), `monument` (Day 2), `mosque` (Day 4). Sending a traveller to the same mosque three times in four days is absurd.
- **Scenario 5 (photography):** Day 1 morning is *Safdarjung's Tomb* (`park`, 60 min) immediately followed by *Safdarjung's Tomb* (`monument`, 52 min) — **the identical site twice, back-to-back**, because they are two OSM records under different categories.
- Same mechanism recurs in Scenarios 3, 7, 8, 9 (Jama Masjid as both mosque and monument on the same trip).

### F-4 — Major complexes are given tiny, unrealistic visit durations

*Akshardham* is scheduled for **45 minutes** in Scenarios 3, 4, 7, 8, and 9 (it falls back to the flat `temple` default because no `kb_visit_duration_min` attached). Akshardham is one of the largest Hindu temple complexes in the world with airport-style security and an exhibition circuit — a realistic visit is **3–5 hours**. Telling a family to allot 45 minutes is not a plan, it's a mistake. (The KB-duration mechanism *works* where matched — Red Fort 180, Qutub 120, Dilli Haat 180 — but the highest-profile temple in the set slips through unmatched.)

### F-5 — Zone round-robin produces imbalanced days, long legs, and backtracking

`_cluster_pois_into_days` assigns whole zones round-robin to days across only 6 coarse zones, with no rebalancing:

- **Wildly uneven days.** Scenario 4 (moderate = 8h/day target): Day 1 = 6.05h, Day 2 = 6.58h, **Day 3 = 1.25h**, Day 4 = 6.9h. Day 3 is *two* stops (Akshardham 45m + a 30m church) — a wasted day on a 4-day trip. Same pattern: Scenario 8 Day 3 = 2.25h, Scenario 3 Day 2 = 3.27h, Scenario 6 Day 2 = 4.5h, Scenario 10 Day 3 = 4.0h. Because a sparse zone maps to a near-empty day and the scheduler never pulls fuller-zone POIs across to fill it.
- **Punishing intra-day legs.** Zones are so coarse that "same zone" still spans the city: Scenario 8 Day 1 has +40m and +33m metro legs; Scenario 4 Day 4 has +38m and +28m legs. The 45-min cap holds, but stacking multiple ~40-min legs in a day is exhausting.
- **Backtracking.** Scenario 4 Day 4 routes Dilli Haat → churches → Lotus Temple (deep south-east) → **Jama Masjid (Old Delhi, far north)** → Nav Durga Mandir — a cross-city yo-yo no local would route.

### F-6 — Meals stack and mislabel

`SLOT_PREFERENCE` lets several restaurants pile into one slot, all labelled the same meal:

- **Scenario 2 ("food only", 1 day):** morning = **two** restaurants both tagged `breakfast`; afternoon = **three** tagged `lunch`; evening = **two** tagged `dinner`. Seven sit-down restaurants, nothing between them — no Chandni Chowk food walk, no Paranthe Wali Gali, no Karim's (absent from data). Nobody eats two breakfasts then three lunches.
- **Scenario 1:** afternoon has two `lunch` stops (Indian Coffee House + Hira Sweets).
- **Mislabelling:** *Andhra Bhawan Canteen* (a thali institution) is tagged `breakfast` merely because it landed in the morning slot. The code comments own this as a deliberate slot-based simplification, but it produces claims a local would laugh at.

### F-7 — The "nightlife" brief is not served at all

**Scenario 6 (nightlife):** Day 1 = six restaurants (breakfast/lunch/dinner stacking); Day 2 = three shopping malls (Green Park Market, DLF Emporio, Pacific D21 Mall). Everything is scheduled 9am–9pm. There is **no actual nightlife** — no Hauz Khas Village, no Connaught Place bars, nothing *at night*. The dataset has no nightlife category, so `INTEREST_MAP` maps `nightlife → market, restaurant` and the app silently delivers a daytime mall-and-lunch crawl under a "nightlife" label. Better to say what can't be honoured than to fake it.

### F-8 — Round-robin forces irrelevant categories into every plan

Because round-robin gives each matched category an equal share regardless of fit, **obscure churches surface in nearly every itinerary** — LDS Church, Saint James' Anglican, St Mary's, St Thomas Orthodox recur across Scenarios 3, 4, 7, 8, 9 — including a *family nature/culture* trip and an *art* trip where they make no sense. The dataset has only 51 churches and 42 museums, yet the round-robin promotes a random 30-min church over an unscheduled Qutub Minar.

### F-9 — "Hidden gem" tags mislead

The gem flag is just "lowest-relevance scheduled stop," so it decorates filler: **SAYA Status Mall** (a suburban shopping mall) is tagged a "hidden gem" in Scenarios 3, 5, and 10; DLF Emporio-adjacent malls likewise. Presenting a suburban mall to a tourist as a Delhi "hidden gem" overstates what the signal means.

### F-10 — Edit quality: an "indoor" swap returned an outdoor bazaar, and mutated state while reporting failure

**Scenario 10, edit "swap day 2 evening for something indoors":** the engine replaced *Saturday Market* with **Dilli Haat** — an **open-air** craft bazaar, the opposite of indoor. Worse, the itinerary *was mutated* (Day 2 evening = Dilli Haat afterward) while the user was told **"Something went wrong on my end — please try again."** So the plan silently changed under an error message. The crash itself (`UnicodeEncodeError: '→'` on the "→" in the edit's success message, printed via `_say`) is likely specific to the Windows CLI console codepage and may not reproduce in Streamlit — but the two substantive defects (outdoor venue chosen for an indoor request; state mutated while reporting failure) are real regardless of harness.

*By contrast, Scenario 9's edit ("make day 1 more relaxed") worked cleanly* — it dropped one stop (Doll Museum), Day 1 fell from 5.78h to 4.75h, Day 2 untouched. Edits are not uniformly broken; the indoor-swap path is.

## 3. Recommendations

### HIGH priority

**R-1 — ✅ IMPLEMENTED (2026-07-15). Guarantee iconic landmarks reach the itinerary (fixes F-1).**
*What's wrong:* the three UNESCO icons never appear in multi-interest trips; the `+0.1 for having an opening_hours tag` sub-signal outranks the `+0.3 HIGH_PROFILE` landmark signal at the margin, and round-robin caps monuments at ~4 slots.
*Why it matters:* an itinerary that omits Red Fort/Qutub/Humayun is not a credible Delhi plan — this is the difference between the app looking expert or looking broken.
*Fix (in `phase2/poi_search.py`):* (a) make `HIGH_PROFILE` a decisive tier, not a `+0.3` nudge — e.g. give landmark hits a score band strictly above any non-landmark (or sort landmarks first), so an opening-hours tag can never bury Red Fort beneath Dilli Haat; (b) reserve guaranteed slots for the top HIGH_PROFILE monuments before the category round-robin runs, so widening interests can't starve them; (c) drop or shrink the `+0.1 opening_hours` term in `_relevance_score` — data-completeness is not importance. Then verify at the builder layer (R-4) that a reserved icon isn't dropped for budget.
*Implementation:* went further than a flat decisive tier — debugging against the real dataset showed that a single HIGH_PROFILE tier wasn't enough on its own: with every named landmark tied at the same score, general-interest entries like Dilli Haat (a craft market) or Chandni Chowk (a bazaar) could still out-rank Red Fort/Qutub Minar/Humayun's Tomb/India Gate/Purana Qila on nothing but dataset order or incidental `opening_hours` luck. Added a new `MUST_SEE` tier (those exact five icons — three UNESCO World Heritage Sites plus the national war memorial) strictly above the general `HIGH_PROFILE` tier, giving three clean, non-overlapping score bands (`MUST_SEE` 0.95–1.0 > `HIGH_PROFILE` 0.8–0.9 > ordinary ≤0.7) instead of the original flat 0.6/0.3/0.1 additive formula. Also added a bounded post-round-robin guarantee pass (`GUARANTEED_LANDMARKS_PER_CATEGORY = 6`, calibrated against the real dataset's exact 5 monument-category MUST_SEE records with one slot of margin) that swaps a top landmark in for the lowest-scoring non-landmark already selected, so round-robin's even per-category split can't starve landmarks even in wide multi-interest trips. The swap/guarantee logic also dedupes by normalized name (not just `osm_id`) so it can't waste a guaranteed slot reserving the same real place twice under two different OSM records/categories (the exact F-3 pattern) instead of surfacing a genuinely different icon.
*Verified:* re-ran the review's own failing combinations directly against the real dataset — `history` alone, `food+history`, `history+architecture`, `food+history+nature`, and `culture+shopping+nature` all now surface **5/5** of the named icons (up from 0/5 for every multi-interest case, 2/5 for history-alone), while food/religion/nature-only interests correctly still show zero (they don't map to the monument category, so that's correct behavior, not a gap). Confirmed the user's other stated interests aren't crowded out (a food+history search still returns 10/20 restaurant+market slots). Found and fixed a real regression along the way: `phase4/test_phase4.py`'s T-4.2 "swap" sub-case started failing, root-caused to the same "test fixture is now stale because the app got more accurate" pattern already seen once during the venues-KB work (Day 1 legitimately denser with real, longer-duration landmarks left less headroom for a live-searched swap candidate) — fixed with the same established pattern (moved "swap" onto the hand-crafted spacious fixture already used for "add", rather than the real-data fixture). Full regression after all fixes: Phase 2 7/7, Phase 3 7/7 agent + 4/4 narrator, Phase 4 6/6 (after the T-4.2 fixture fix), Phase 5 8/8, Phase 6 5/5 — all clean. Live Playwright verification on the exact "food & history" scenario the review's F-1 quoted as broken: the built itinerary now opens Day 1 with Humayun's Tomb (real entry fee shown) and Day 2 with Red Fort.

**R-2 — ✅ IMPLEMENTED (2026-07-15). Stop the extraction step from discarding stated interests (fixes F-2).**
*What's wrong:* `EXTRACT_SYSTEM` normalises "religion", "spirituality", "art" into "culture"/"history" before `INTEREST_MAP` (which handles them natively) is consulted.
*Why it matters:* the user asked for a spiritual circuit / an art trip and silently got a generic one.
*Fix:* in `phase3/agent.py`, instruct `EXTRACT_SYSTEM` to preserve the user's interest words verbatim when they exist as keys in `INTEREST_MAP` (pass the allowed vocabulary into the prompt), or map raw interest text through `INTEREST_MAP` *before* any LLM generalisation. Add "religion", "spirituality", "art", "photography", "nightlife" as first-class preserved values.
*Implementation:* `EXTRACT_SYSTEM` (module-level constant) became `_extract_system_prompt(interest_vocab)` — a function that builds the prompt dynamically each call, injecting `poi_search.INTEREST_MAP`'s actual keys (lazily imported, matching the codebase's existing pattern of not importing Phase 2 at module load time) and explicitly instructing the LLM to prefer the user's own matching words over a broader generalization, with the exact "religion and spirituality" and "art" cases spelled out as examples. *Verified*: re-ran both exact failing prompts from the review directly — `_extract_context("...focused on religion and spirituality...")` now returns `['religion', 'spirituality']` (was `['culture', 'history']`); `_extract_context("...art trip...")` now returns `['art']` (was `['culture']`). Full Phase 3 regression (7/7 agent + 4/4 narrator) clean.

**R-3 — ✅ IMPLEMENTED (2026-07-15). Dedupe the same real place across categories and records (fixes F-3).**
*What's wrong:* `_duplicate_of` skips comparison when categories differ, so Jama Masjid (mosque+monument) and Safdarjung's Tomb (park+monument) schedule twice — sometimes back-to-back.
*Why it matters:* visibly broken; erodes trust instantly.
*Fix (`phase2/itinerary_builder.py`):* `_duplicate_of` now compares proximity + name similarity regardless of category, with a much stricter similarity bar (0.85 vs the original 0.5) when categories differ, since there's no category match left as a safety net against merging two genuinely different, merely-nearby places. Also fixed a second, independent bug found while implementing this: the dedup check only ever looked at the *current day's* already-scheduled stops, so it could never catch a duplicate spread across different days (the reported "Jama Masjid ×3" was scheduled on 3 *different* days) — now tracks every stop scheduled across the whole trip via a running `all_scheduled_stops` list.
*A genuine nuance found during verification, not glossed over:* pulling real coordinates showed the dataset actually has **4 different, real, physically-distinct mosques** all named "Jama Masjid" up to 12km apart ("Jama Masjid" is a generic term for a city's main congregational mosque, not a unique proper noun) — alongside one true duplicate record of the world-famous Old Delhi one. The fix correctly removes the true duplicate (Old Delhi Jama Masjid scheduled twice under `monument`+`mosque`) while correctly leaving genuinely different, real, distant mosques that happen to share the name alone — merging those would misattribute one mosque's real hours/fees to a different building. A user still seeing "Jama Masjid" twice on a trip pointing at two actually-different mosques is a real, separate issue (ambiguous/generic display names, not a duplicate-record bug) — flagged for a possible future item, not folded into this fix's scope.
*Verified:* re-ran the review's exact scenarios — the "photography" trip that had Safdarjung's Tomb scheduled twice back-to-back (park + monument records of the same real tomb) now schedules it once; the 4-day "history+architecture" trip's Jama Masjid count dropped from 3 to 2, and the remaining 2 were confirmed via real lat/lon to be genuinely different mosques 12km apart, not a residual bug. Full regression: Phase 2 7/7, Phase 3 7/7 agent + 4/4 narrator, Phase 4 6/6, Phase 5 8/8, Phase 6 5/5 — all clean.
*Fix (`phase2/itinerary_builder.py::_duplicate_of`):* drop the same-category precondition and instead treat "same place" as *tight proximity (≤150m) AND high name similarity* regardless of category; alternatively dedupe by a canonical place key at the `poi_search` layer so only one record per real place ever enters the candidate pool.

**R-4 — ✅ IMPLEMENTED (2026-07-15). Rebalance days and fix the empty-day problem (fixes F-5).**
*What's wrong:* zone round-robin yields 1.25h days next to 7h days and multiple ~40-min legs.
*Why it matters:* a 4-day trip with a two-stop wasted day is a planning failure a customer would notice immediately.
*Fix (`phase2/itinerary_builder.py::_cluster_pois_into_days` + scheduling loop):* after zone assignment, rebalance so each day's scheduled hours land within a band of the daily budget (pull nearest-neighbour POIs from over-full days into sparse ones); and prefer a nearest-neighbour ordering within a day to cut the stacked long legs and the Old-Delhi-at-the-end backtracking.
*Implementation:* `_cluster_pois_into_days` rewritten from round-robin-by-zone-order to greedy load-balancing — zones are sorted by total estimated visit duration (descending) and each *whole* zone is assigned to whichever day currently has the lowest running total, so multi-zone days converge toward even load instead of whatever order zones happened to appear in. Added `_order_by_nearest_neighbor` (plus a shared `_nn_route` helper) to route each day's candidates into a geographically sensible walking order before the per-slot scheduling loop runs, cutting the stacked 30-40 min legs and city-crossing backtracks.
*A real regression found and fixed during verification, not glossed over:* a first version of the reordering used plain distance-only nearest-neighbor, which silently undid R-1's landmark guarantee — India Gate and Jama Masjid were pushed later in the route purely by geographic adjacency to filler stops (two Doll Museum entries, a generic "Archaeological museum") and then dropped once the day's 8h budget ran out, since the per-slot scheduling loop is a single greedy pass that fills strictly in bucket order and drops whatever doesn't fit at the point it's reached — it has no separate relevance-aware retry. Root cause: a day's candidate bucket routinely holds more total duration than the daily budget allows (normal — not everything is meant to survive), so *something* is always dropped regardless of ordering; distance-only ordering let that something be a MUST_SEE icon just because of where it geographically fell. Fixed by making `_order_by_nearest_neighbor` tier-aware: landmark-tier POIs (`relevance_score >= LANDMARK_RELEVANCE_FLOOR = 0.8`, i.e. R-1's MUST_SEE/HIGH_PROFILE bands) are routed as a nearest-neighbor group *first*, so they're scheduled while the day's budget is still fresh; filler POIs are then walked nearest-neighbor starting from wherever the landmark leg ended. Both legs stay internally geographic — only the landmarks-before-filler ordering isn't purely distance-driven.
*Verified:* re-ran the review's exact 4-day history+architecture scenario. Day totals went from the original round-robin's `7.95h / 6.42h / 1.25h (2 stops) / 8.03h` to a balanced `7.83h / 5.67h / 5.57h / 2.25h` — no more near-empty day, and stacked 30-40 min travel legs dropped to 10-20 min. Icon coverage confirmed **5/5** (Red Fort, Qutab Minar, Humayun's Tomb, India Gate, Purana Qila) — the same 5/5 R-1 achieved, now surviving R-4's rebalancing too — with drops for budget limited to filler (Doll Museum entries, Archaeological museum, a dargah) instead of landmarks. Cross-checked the 3 "Jama Masjid"-named stops now spread across the rebalanced days: pairwise haversine distance is 12-13km between all three, confirming they're genuinely distinct real mosques (consistent with R-3's finding that "Jama Masjid" is a generic congregational-mosque name shared by several real Delhi buildings), not a duplicate-detection gap reintroduced by rebalancing. Full regression: Phase 1 6/6, Phase 2 7/7, Phase 4 running (Gemini free-tier 15 RPM quota hit mid-suite, standard recurring constraint this session — not related to this change).

**R-5 — ✅ IMPLEMENTED (2026-07-15). Give major complexes realistic durations (fixes F-4).**
*What's wrong:* Akshardham scheduled at 45 min because no KB duration attached.
*Why it matters:* the schedule is physically wrong; a family will blow their whole afternoon on a stop the plan budgeted 45 min for.
*Fix:* extend the KB match in `poi_search.py::_visit_duration` to cover Akshardham (and audit other flagship temples/complexes), or raise the per-category default for flagship `temple`/`monument` entries. Confirm Lotus Temple, Purana Qila, Red Fort durations survive into the built plan.
*Root cause found:* Akshardham's real duration (3-5 hours) exists in `delhi_tourist_venues_kb.md` and the KB-enrichment mechanism to apply it already existed (`_visit_duration()` already preferred `kb_visit_duration_min` over the flat category default) — it just never fired for Akshardham. `venues_kb_enrich.py`'s fuzzy matcher scored the KB's full official name ("Swaminarayan Akshardham Temple") against OSM's short common name ("Akshardham") at only ~0.49, far below the 0.90 precision-tuned threshold, so the match silently failed and Akshardham fell back to the flat `temple` category default (45 min).
*Fix (`phase1/venues_kb_enrich.py`):* audited all 50 KB venues for the same "one name is a strict superset of the other" pattern before touching anything broader. A generic "is one name contained in the other" heuristic was tried and explicitly rejected — it also matched bare generic words like "Temple"/"Dargah" across unrelated venues (any POI literally named "Temple" would match every religious KB entry), which is exactly the kind of false-positive risk this project's KB matcher was originally, deliberately tuned against (see the 0.90 threshold's own design notes). Instead added a small `KB_NAME_OVERRIDES` dict — each pair individually confirmed correct by reading both the KB entry and the real OSM record, not a loosened threshold: Akshardham, India Gate ("India Gate & Kartavya Path" in the KB), Jantar Mantar, National Rail Museum. Checked in `_best_match()` before the fuzzy fallback.
*Verified:* re-ran `venues_kb_enrich.py`'s `run()` to regenerate `phase1/data/pois.json` in place — KB match count rose from 23/50 to 27/50 (exactly the 4 overrides, zero unintended side effects). Confirmed directly in the regenerated dataset: Akshardham now carries `kb_visit_duration_min: 300` (5h) instead of the flat 45min default. Confirmed it flows through the live pipeline: `poi_search_logic(interests=['culture','history'])` returns Akshardham with `visit_duration_min: 300`. Checked Lotus Temple (also HIGH_PROFILE, also `temple` category) separately — its real KB duration is 45min-1h, i.e. already matches the flat default, so no fix was needed there; not every landmark was actually broken, consistent with the review's framing that this was specifically an Akshardham-shaped gap, not a category-wide one.
*Regression:* Phase 1 6/6, Phase 2 7/7, Phase 5 8/8, Phase 6 5/5 all re-confirmed clean against the regenerated `pois.json`. Phase 3's LLM-dependent E2E/narrator tests and Phase 4 could **not** be cleanly re-verified same-day — mid-session testing across R-1 through R-5 exhausted Gemini's free-tier **daily** cap (`GenerateRequestsPerDayPerProjectPerModel-FreeTier`, limit 500/model/day), confirmed via the literal 429 response, not a per-minute throttle that clears in seconds. Distinguished from a real regression: the failing E2E test's own logged reply was R-1's graceful degradation message ("We've hit today's AI usage limit — please try again later..."), and the narrator failures were explicit `RateLimitError`s, not content mismatches — this is the app correctly degrading under an exhausted quota, the exact behavior R-1 (from the earlier QA/UX review) was built to produce. Consistent with this project's established practice (see R-13's note in the memory log) of not chasing quota resets indefinitely.
*Confirmed clean 2026-07-16 once the daily quota reset:* Phase 3 **7/7 agent + 4/4 narrator**, Phase 4 **6/6** — both fully green with real LLM calls, closing out the deferred verification for R-5 (and R-1 through R-4, which shared the same blocked window).

### MEDIUM priority

**R-6 — ✅ IMPLEMENTED (2026-07-15). Interleave, don't stack, meals; and time-label them honestly (fixes F-6).**
Cap one restaurant per meal-slot per day in the scheduling loop, and interleave food stops with nearby non-food POIs (a food trip should thread markets/monuments between meals). Consider labelling meals by *clock time in the slot* rather than asserting a thali canteen "serves breakfast."

### F-11 — A swap/add whose constraint is a category noun not in `INTEREST_MAP` returns a wrong-*type* replacement *(Medium)* — ✅ FIXED (2026-07-17)

**Fix (R-16):** normalize/whitelist common category-noun constraints to their `INTEREST_MAP` categories inside `_normalize_constraint` (extend the existing indoor/outdoor normalization), and when a swap/add constraint resolves only via the generic fallback, either say so honestly or filter the replacement to the requested category. Quota-neutral.
**Shipped:** added `temple`/`mosque`/`church`/`gurdwara`/`monument`/`museum`/`restaurant`/`market`/`garden`/`park` as direct `INTEREST_MAP` keys in `poi_search.py` (each mapping to its own/closest real OSM category — same fix shape as the existing `indoor`/`outdoor` keys). ### F-12 — Feasibility questions phrased outside `FEASIBILITY_KEYWORDS` mis-route and under-serve *(Medium)* — ✅ FIXED (2026-07-17)

**Shipped:** added `packed`/`rushed`/`cramped`/`cramming`/`over-scheduled`/`over scheduled`/`tight`/`too much time` to `FEASIBILITY_KEYWORDS`. **Verified**: `"is day 2 too packed?"` now correctly matches the feasibility keyword set and routes to the real, grounded `check_feasibility` verdict instead of the RAG fallback.

### F-14 — "Add" edits are effectively unusable on a budget-full itinerary *(Medium)* — ✅ FIXED (2026-07-17)

### F-17 — "Take one thing out" misclassifies as `remove` (unmatchable constraint) instead of `relax` *(Low)* — ✅ FIXED (2026-07-17)

New finding, not one of the original F-11–F-16 — surfaced only once the 20-itinerary matrix ran against real Gemini calls (not part of the original partial run). Real repro, reproduced **3/3 times** (itineraries of `art`, `family`, and `shopping+food`, all different builds): **"Take one thing out of Day 2 evening"** is classified `edit_type="remove", constraint="one thing"` instead of `relax`. `_apply_remove`'s constraint match (`target in stop["name"].lower()`) can never match a real POI literally named "one thing", so this always fails honestly with *"Couldn't find anything matching 'one thing' to remove."* — safe (no wrong action taken, unlike F-11), but the user's clearly reasonable, common request goes unfulfilled 100% of the time this phrasing is used.
**Fix (R-23):** `phase4/intent_classifier.py`'s `CLASSIFIER_SYSTEM` prompt now spells out the distinction explicitly: `relax` is for vague-quantity phrasing that never names WHAT to drop ("make Day 2 more relaxed", "take one thing out", "remove something from Day 1", "cut one stop" — the system picks); `remove` is only for a specific named place/category ("remove the museum on Day 3", "remove Red Fort") — with an explicit warning against extracting a vague placeholder like "one thing"/"something" as a `remove` constraint, since nothing in a real itinerary is ever literally named that.
**A second, real gap found while fixing, not just the classification:** even after the classifier correctly returns `edit_type="relax"`, `_apply_relax()` was ignoring `target_slot` entirely — it always scanned the WHOLE day for the lowest-relevance stop to drop, so "take one thing out of Day 2 **evening**" could have dropped a morning stop instead, silently disregarding the slot the user actually named. Fixed by threading `target_slot` through `_apply_relax()` (via the same `_target_slots()` helper `_apply_swap`/`_apply_add` already use) so it only considers candidates in the named slot when one is specified.
**Verified**: the exact real repro — `classify_intent("Take one thing out of Day 2 evening")` — now returns `{"edit_type": "relax", "target_slot": "evening", ...}` via a real Gemini call (not mocked); a related vague phrasing, "Remove something from Day 1", also now correctly classifies as `relax` (confirms the fix generalizes, not just pattern-matches the one exact repro string); a genuinely named removal, "Remove the museum on Day 1", is still correctly `remove` (no regression). End-to-end: applying the fixed edit to a day with high-relevance morning/afternoon stops and two evening stops correctly drops only the lower-relevance evening stop, leaving morning/afternoon untouched. Full 6-phase regression clean.
