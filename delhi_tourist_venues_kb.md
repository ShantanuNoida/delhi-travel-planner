## Section 1: Religious Places

### 1. Swaminarayan Akshardham Temple

- **Category:** Religious (secondary: cultural, family/entertainment)
- **Tags:** spiritual, architecture, family-friendly, cultural-show, photography-restricted, evening-visit, wheelchair-accessible
- **Why It Is Famous:** Akshardham is a large Hindu temple complex on the eastern bank of the Yamuna, inaugurated in 2005 by the BAPS Swaminarayan organisation. The central monument is built of pink sandstone and white marble without structural steel, featuring around 20,000 carved figures of deities, sages, flora and fauna. It entered the Guinness World Records as the world's largest comprehensive Hindu temple at the time of certification. The complex includes themed exhibitions on Indian culture, a boat ride through 10,000 years of Indian heritage, and the Sahaj Anand water-and-light show held after sunset. Its landscaped gardens and Yagnapurush Kund step-well amphitheatre are additional draws. Mobile phones, cameras and electronic items are not permitted inside.
- **Timings:** Open Tuesday to Sunday, approximately 10:00 AM – 8:00 PM (last entry around 6:30 PM); closed on Mondays. Water show timings vary by sunset; verify locally.
- **Entry Fee:** Temple entry free for all; exhibitions approx. ₹250 (adults), ₹150 (children 4–11), free for children under 4; water show ticketed separately (approx. ₹100–₹150). Verify locally.
- **Recommended Visit Duration:** 3–5 hours (with exhibitions and water show)
- **Best Time to Visit:** Late afternoon into evening (to include the water show); October–March for pleasant weather
- **How to Reach:**
  - **Nearest Metro Station:** Akshardham (Blue Line), approx. 350–500 m / 5–8 minutes' walk to the entrance
  - **Nearest Bus Stop:** Akshardham Mandir bus stop on NH-9/Noida Link Road; served by multiple DTC routes towards Noida and Mayur Vihar (verify current route numbers locally)
  - **Other Options:** Auto-rickshaws readily available; large paid parking on site for private vehicles
- **Suitable For:** families, kids, elderly, spiritual travellers, architecture enthusiasts, first-time visitors
- **Nearby Attractions:** National Zoological Park (No. 45), Purana Qila (No. 20), Waste to Wonder Park (No. 46)

### 2. Jama Masjid

- **Category:** Religious (secondary: historical)
- **Tags:** heritage, mughal-architecture, spiritual, photography, old-delhi, free-entry, street-food-nearby
- **Why It Is Famous:** Jama Masjid (Masjid-i-Jahan-Numa) is one of the largest mosques in India, commissioned by Mughal emperor Shah Jahan and completed in 1656. Built of red sandstone and white marble, it features three great gates, two 40-metre-high minarets and a courtyard that can accommodate around 25,000 worshippers. It served as the principal congregational mosque of the Mughal capital Shahjahanabad and remains an active place of worship. Visitors can climb the southern minaret (ticketed) for views over Old Delhi. Its position facing the Red Fort anchors the historic core of Old Delhi, surrounded by the bazaars and food lanes of the walled city.
- **Timings:** Generally open to visitors approx. 7:00 AM – 12:00 PM and 1:30 PM – 6:30 PM daily; closed to tourists during prayer times, especially Friday afternoon prayers. Verify locally.
- **Entry Fee:** Entry free; photography/videography charge approx. ₹200–₹300; minaret climb approx. ₹100. Modest dress required; robes available at the gate. Verify locally.
- **Recommended Visit Duration:** 45 minutes – 1.5 hours
- **Best Time to Visit:** Early morning or late afternoon; October–March; avoid Friday midday
- **How to Reach:**
  - **Nearest Metro Station:** Jama Masjid (Violet Line), approx. 300–500 m / 5 minutes' walk
  - **Nearest Bus Stop:** Jama Masjid bus stop on Netaji Subhash Marg; served by numerous DTC routes through Old Delhi (verify current route numbers locally)
  - **Other Options:** Cycle-rickshaws throughout Old Delhi; autos at Netaji Subhash Marg; private parking very limited — metro strongly recommended
- **Suitable For:** history buffs, photographers, solo travellers, budget travellers, spiritual travellers
- **Nearby Attractions:** Red Fort (No. 16), Karim's (No. 35), Chandni Chowk Food Street (No. 33)

### 3. Gurudwara Bangla Sahib

- **Category:** Religious
- **Tags:** spiritual, free-entry, langar, sikh-heritage, central-delhi, family-friendly, wheelchair-accessible
- **Why It Is Famous:** Bangla Sahib is the most prominent Sikh gurudwara in Delhi, associated with the eighth Sikh Guru, Guru Har Krishan, who stayed at this site in 1664 while tending to victims of a smallpox and cholera epidemic. The complex features a gilded dome, a large sacred tank (sarovar) whose water is considered holy by devotees, and continuous recitation of the Guru Granth Sahib. Its community kitchen (langar) serves free vegetarian meals to thousands of visitors daily regardless of faith, and visitors may volunteer in the kitchen. The gurudwara is open to people of all religions and operates around the clock. Head covering (scarves provided) and shoe removal are mandatory.
- **Timings:** Open 24 hours daily; langar typically served approx. 9:00 AM – 3:00 PM and 7:00 PM – 10:00 PM. Verify locally.
- **Entry Fee:** Free
- **Recommended Visit Duration:** 1–1.5 hours
- **Best Time to Visit:** Early morning or evening for prayers and lit-up views; year-round
- **How to Reach:**
  - **Nearest Metro Station:** Patel Chowk (Yellow Line), approx. 900 m – 1 km / 12 minutes' walk; Rajiv Chowk (Blue/Yellow Lines) approx. 1.3 km; Shivaji Stadium (Airport Express/Orange Line) approx. 700 m
  - **Nearest Bus Stop:** Gurudwara Bangla Sahib stop on Ashoka Road/Baba Kharak Singh Marg; multiple DTC routes via Connaught Place (verify current route numbers locally)
  - **Other Options:** Autos widely available; limited free parking near the complex
- **Suitable For:** families, elderly, spiritual travellers, budget travellers, solo travellers
- **Nearby Attractions:** Connaught Place (No. 29), Jantar Mantar (No. 21), Sacred Heart Cathedral (No. 8)

### 4. Lotus Temple (Baháʼí House of Worship)

- **Category:** Religious (secondary: architecture landmark)
- **Tags:** architecture, spiritual, free-entry, photography, meditation, family-friendly, wheelchair-accessible
- **Why It Is Famous:** The Lotus Temple, completed in 1986, is the Baháʼí House of Worship for the Indian subcontinent, designed by Iranian-Canadian architect Fariborz Sahba. Its structure of 27 free-standing marble-clad "petals" arranged in clusters of three forms a lotus flower, a symbol common to several Indian religions. The temple is open to people of all faiths; there are no idols, sermons or rituals, and silence is maintained inside the central prayer hall, which seats about 1,300 people. It has won multiple international architectural awards and is frequently cited among the most-visited buildings in the world. The surrounding gardens and nine reflecting pools enhance the lotus-on-water effect.
- **Timings:** Open Tuesday to Sunday, approx. 9:00 AM – 7:00 PM (summer) and 9:00 AM – 5:30 PM (winter); closed on Mondays.
- **Entry Fee:** Free
- **Recommended Visit Duration:** 45 minutes – 1 hour
- **Best Time to Visit:** Late afternoon towards sunset; October–March
- **How to Reach:**
  - **Nearest Metro Station:** Kalkaji Mandir (Violet and Magenta Lines), approx. 500–600 m / 8 minutes' walk
  - **Nearest Bus Stop:** Nehru Place Terminal (approx. 1 km), a major DTC hub with routes across Delhi (verify current route numbers locally)
  - **Other Options:** Autos from Nehru Place; paid parking available near the entrance
- **Suitable For:** families, couples, solo travellers, architecture enthusiasts, elderly, budget travellers
- **Nearby Attractions:** ISKCON Temple (No. 5), Kalkaji area markets, Garden of Five Senses (No. 41)

### 5. ISKCON Temple, East of Kailash (Sri Sri Radha Parthasarathi Mandir)

- **Category:** Religious
- **Tags:** spiritual, krishna-temple, vegetarian-food, free-entry, evening-aarti, family-friendly, cultural-show
- **Why It Is Famous:** Opened in 1998, this is the flagship Delhi temple of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness, dedicated to Radha and Krishna as Radha Parthasarathi. The hilltop complex at Hare Krishna Hills combines traditional temple architecture with modern facilities, including multimedia shows on the Bhagavad Gita and Vedic culture. It houses one of the world's largest printed sacred books, a giant Bhagavad Gita unveiled in 2019. Daily aartis, kirtan singing and festivals such as Janmashtami draw large crowds. Govinda's, the temple's pure-vegetarian restaurant, is a destination in its own right for sattvic food.
- **Timings:** Open daily, approx. 4:30 AM – 1:00 PM and 4:00 PM – 9:00 PM; darshan closed in the afternoon. Verify locally.
- **Entry Fee:** Free; multimedia shows ticketed (approx. ₹50–₹100). Verify locally.
- **Recommended Visit Duration:** 1–2 hours
- **Best Time to Visit:** Evening aarti (around 7:00 PM); festival days for atmosphere, non-festival days for calm
- **How to Reach:**
  - **Nearest Metro Station:** Nehru Place (Violet Line), approx. 1 km / 12–15 minutes' walk; Kailash Colony (Violet Line) approx. 1.2 km
  - **Nearest Bus Stop:** Nehru Place Terminal, a major DTC hub (verify current route numbers locally)
  - **Other Options:** Autos from Nehru Place; on-site parking available
- **Suitable For:** families, spiritual travellers, foodies (vegetarian), kids, solo travellers
- **Nearby Attractions:** Lotus Temple (No. 4), Kalkaji Mandir, Garden of Five Senses (No. 41)

### 6. Chhatarpur Temple (Shri Adya Katyayani Shaktipeeth)

- **Category:** Religious
- **Tags:** spiritual, temple-complex, free-entry, marble-architecture, navratri, family-friendly, south-delhi
- **Why It Is Famous:** The Chhatarpur temple complex, founded in 1974 by Baba Sant Nagpal, is dedicated to Goddess Katyayani, an incarnation of Durga. Spread over roughly 60–70 acres, it was among the largest temple complexes in India before Akshardham was built. The complex contains more than a dozen shrines built in a blend of South Indian and North Indian architectural styles, executed largely in white marble with intricate jaali (lattice) work. The main Katyayani shrine opens fully during the twice-yearly Navratri festivals, when the complex hosts large fairs and continuous darshan. A room preserved as the living quarters of the founder and a large sacred tree tied with devotees' wish-threads are notable features.
- **Timings:** Open daily, approx. 6:00 AM – 10:00 PM; extended hours during Navratri. Verify locally.
- **Entry Fee:** Free
- **Recommended Visit Duration:** 1–1.5 hours
- **Best Time to Visit:** Morning hours; Navratri (March–April and September–October) for festival atmosphere
- **How to Reach:**
  - **Nearest Metro Station:** Chhatarpur (Yellow Line), approx. 500–800 m / 8–10 minutes' walk
  - **Nearest Bus Stop:** Chhatarpur Mandir stop on Main Chhatarpur Road; DTC routes towards Mehrauli (verify current route numbers locally)
  - **Other Options:** Autos and e-rickshaws from the metro station; on-site parking available
- **Suitable For:** families, spiritual travellers, elderly, budget travellers
- **Nearby Attractions:** Qutub Minar (No. 17), Mehrauli Archaeological Park (No. 25), Garden of Five Senses (No. 41)

### 7. Hazrat Nizamuddin Dargah

- **Category:** Religious (secondary: cultural, historical)
- **Tags:** sufi-heritage, qawwali, spiritual, free-entry, evening-visit, old-world-lanes, photography
- **Why It Is Famous:** This is the dargah (shrine) of Hazrat Nizamuddin Auliya (1238–1325), one of the most revered Sufi saints of the Chishti order, visited by devotees of all faiths for over seven centuries. The shrine complex also contains the graves of the poet-musician Amir Khusrau, credited with shaping Hindustani music and early qawwali, and the Mughal princess Jahanara Begum. Thursday-evening qawwali performances in the courtyard are a celebrated living tradition of devotional music. The shrine sits within the dense Nizamuddin Basti, whose lanes are lined with flower sellers, kebab shops and historic structures. The precinct is closely linked to nearby Humayun's Tomb, built in the saint's vicinity because burial near a saint was considered auspicious.
- **Timings:** Open daily, approx. 5:00 AM – 10:30 PM; qawwali typically after evening prayers (around 6:00–7:30 PM), best on Thursdays. Verify locally.
- **Entry Fee:** Free (donations optional); head covering required; shoe deposit small tip customary
- **Recommended Visit Duration:** 1–1.5 hours
- **Best Time to Visit:** Thursday evening for qawwali; October–March
- **How to Reach:**
  - **Nearest Metro Station:** Hazrat Nizamuddin (Pink Line), approx. 1 km / 12–15 minutes' walk; JLN Stadium (Violet Line) approx. 1.5 km
  - **Nearest Bus Stop:** Nizamuddin Basti/Lodhi Road stops on Mathura Road; multiple DTC routes (verify current route numbers locally)
  - **Other Options:** Autos on Mathura Road and Lodhi Road; no parking inside the basti — walk in from the main road
- **Suitable For:** history buffs, music lovers, solo travellers, photographers, spiritual travellers
- **Nearby Attractions:** Humayun's Tomb (No. 18), Sunder Nursery (No. 40), Khan Market (No. 30)

### 8. Sacred Heart Cathedral

- **Category:** Religious
- **Tags:** church, colonial-architecture, free-entry, christmas, central-delhi, quiet, spiritual
- **Why It Is Famous:** Sacred Heart Cathedral is the principal Roman Catholic cathedral of the Archdiocese of Delhi, completed in the 1930s to a design by British architect Henry Medd as part of the planning of New Delhi. Its Italianate facade with twin towers, curved frontage and tall arched portico makes it one of the most recognisable colonial-era churches in the capital. The interior features a high curved roof, marble altar and broad nave that hosts large congregations. Midnight Mass at Christmas and Easter services draw visitors of all faiths, and the church precinct near Gole Dak Khana includes historic Catholic schools. It offers a quiet contrast to the busier monuments of central Delhi.
- **Timings:** Open daily, approx. 6:30 AM – 6:00 PM; mass timings vary — verify locally. Visitor access limited during services.
- **Entry Fee:** Free
- **Recommended Visit Duration:** 30–45 minutes
- **Best Time to Visit:** Morning on weekdays for quiet; Christmas season for decorations and services
- **How to Reach:**
  - **Nearest Metro Station:** Patel Chowk (Yellow Line), approx. 1–1.2 km / 15 minutes' walk; Shivaji Stadium (Airport Express/Orange Line) approx. 800 m
  - **Nearest Bus Stop:** Gole Dak Khana stop; DTC routes along Baba Kharak Singh Marg and Ashoka Road (verify current route numbers locally)
  - **Other Options:** Autos readily available; limited street parking nearby
- **Suitable For:** solo travellers, couples, history buffs, elderly, spiritual travellers
- **Nearby Attractions:** Gurudwara Bangla Sahib (No. 3), Connaught Place (No. 29), Jantar Mantar (No. 21)

## Section 2: Museums

### 9. National Museum

- **Category:** Museum
- **Tags:** heritage, art-and-artefacts, indoor, air-conditioned, history, central-delhi, monsoon-friendly
- **Why It Is Famous:** The National Museum, established in 1949 and located on Janpath, holds India's premier collection of art and archaeology, with over 200,000 objects spanning about 5,000 years. Highlights include Harappan-civilisation artefacts such as the bronze Dancing Girl, Buddhist relics including remains associated with the Buddha, miniature-painting galleries, sculpture from major Indian dynasties, and arms, textiles and decorative arts. Its Indus Valley gallery is among the most comprehensive anywhere. Audio guides in multiple languages support self-paced visits. Note: relocation of collections has been under discussion as part of the Central Vista redevelopment, so confirm operational status before visiting.
- **Timings:** Open Tuesday to Sunday, approx. 10:00 AM – 6:00 PM; closed on Mondays and national holidays. Verify locally.
- **Entry Fee:** Indian nationals approx. ₹20; foreign nationals approx. ₹650 (with audio guide); students and children discounted; camera charges may apply. Verify locally.
- **Recommended Visit Duration:** 2–3 hours
- **Best Time to Visit:** Weekday mornings; useful indoor option in summer and monsoon
- **How to Reach:**
  - **Nearest Metro Station:** Udyog Bhawan (Yellow Line), approx. 800 m / 10 minutes' walk; Central Secretariat (Yellow/Violet Lines) approx. 1 km
  - **Nearest Bus Stop:** National Museum stop on Janpath; DTC routes along Janpath and Rajpath/Kartavya Path axis (verify current route numbers locally)
  - **Other Options:** Autos readily available; limited paid parking nearby
- **Suitable For:** history buffs, families, students, solo travellers, elderly
- **Nearby Attractions:** India Gate & Kartavya Path (No. 19), Rashtrapati Bhavan (No. 26), National Gallery of Modern Art (No. 11)

### 10. National Rail Museum

- **Category:** Museum (secondary: family/entertainment)
- **Tags:** kid-friendly, trains, outdoor-and-indoor, toy-train-ride, family-friendly, photography, educational
- **Why It Is Famous:** Opened in 1977 in Chanakyapuri, the National Rail Museum documents more than 160 years of Indian railway history across an 11-acre site. Its outdoor yard displays vintage steam, diesel and electric locomotives, royal saloon carriages (including those of Indian princely states), and rolling stock such as the Fairy Queen, one of the world's oldest working steam locomotives. Indoor galleries hold models, signalling equipment and interactive simulators. A ticketed toy-train ride circles the grounds, making it one of Delhi's most popular outings for children. The museum balances technical railway heritage with family-oriented attractions.
- **Timings:** Open Tuesday to Sunday, approx. 10:00 AM – 5:00 PM (last entry approx. 4:30 PM); closed on Mondays and national holidays.
- **Entry Fee:** Approx. ₹50 (adults) and ₹10–₹25 (children) on weekdays; approx. ₹100/₹50 on weekends; rides and simulators ticketed separately. Verify locally.
- **Recommended Visit Duration:** 2–3 hours
- **Best Time to Visit:** Weekday mornings; October–March (much of the display is open-air)
- **How to Reach:**
  - **Nearest Metro Station:** Sir M. Vishweshwaraiah Moti Bagh (Pink Line), approx. 1.5–2 km / auto recommended; Dhaula Kuan (Airport Express) approx. 2.5 km
  - **Nearest Bus Stop:** Rail Museum/Bhutan Embassy stop, Shanti Path–Chanakyapuri routes (verify current route numbers locally)
  - **Other Options:** Autos from Dhaula Kuan or Moti Bagh; on-site parking available
- **Suitable For:** families, kids, train enthusiasts, photographers
- **Nearby Attractions:** Nehru Park (No. 42), Chanakyapuri diplomatic enclave drives, Pradhanmantri Sangrahalaya (No. 14)

### 11. National Gallery of Modern Art (NGMA)

- **Category:** Museum
- **Tags:** art, indoor, air-conditioned, colonial-building, quiet, monsoon-friendly, central-delhi
- **Why It Is Famous:** NGMA, established in 1954 in Jaipur House near India Gate, is India's foremost institution for modern and contemporary art, holding a collection of roughly 17,000 works from the mid-19th century onwards. It houses major holdings of Raja Ravi Varma, Abanindranath and Rabindranath Tagore, Amrita Sher-Gil, Jamini Roy and the Progressive Artists' Group, alongside colonial-era Company paintings. Jaipur House itself is a butterfly-plan former royal residence built for the Maharaja of Jaipur, with a modern extension wing added later. Rotating exhibitions, sculpture gardens and a reference library support deeper engagement. It is one of the quieter major museums, suited to unhurried viewing.
- **Timings:** Open Tuesday to Sunday, approx. 10:00 AM – 6:00 PM (last entry 5:30 PM); closed on Mondays and national holidays.
- **Entry Fee:** Indian nationals approx. ₹20; foreign nationals approx. ₹500; free for students up to a certain level with ID. Verify locally.
- **Recommended Visit Duration:** 1.5–2.5 hours
- **Best Time to Visit:** Weekday afternoons; indoor option for summer and monsoon
- **How to Reach:**
  - **Nearest Metro Station:** Khan Market (Violet Line), approx. 1.5 km / auto or 20 minutes' walk; Central Secretariat (Yellow/Violet Lines) approx. 2 km
  - **Nearest Bus Stop:** India Gate/Sher Shah Road stops; DTC routes along Mathura Road and C-Hexagon (verify current route numbers locally)
  - **Other Options:** Autos readily available; limited parking at Jaipur House
- **Suitable For:** art lovers, couples, solo travellers, students
- **Nearby Attractions:** India Gate & Kartavya Path (No. 19), National Museum (No. 9), Purana Qila (No. 20)

### 12. National Science Centre

- **Category:** Museum (secondary: family/entertainment)
- **Tags:** kid-friendly, interactive, educational, indoor, air-conditioned, monsoon-friendly, science
- **Why It Is Famous:** The National Science Centre, opened in 1992 near Pragati Maidan, is one of the largest science museums in Asia, run by the National Council of Science Museums. Its multi-storey galleries cover human biology, prehistoric life (with animatronic dinosaurs), information technology, heritage science in ancient India, and hands-on "Fun Science" halls where exhibits are designed to be touched and operated. A 3D show, innovation hub and regular science demonstrations make it strongly oriented towards school-age visitors. The building's atrium features a large Energy Ball kinetic sculpture. It is a reliable full- or half-day indoor destination for families in any season.
- **Timings:** Open daily, approx. 9:30 AM – 6:00 PM (last entry approx. 5:00–5:30 PM); closed on Holi and Diwali. Verify locally.
- **Entry Fee:** Approx. ₹95 (adults); discounted for students/children; 3D shows and special galleries ticketed separately. Verify locally.
- **Recommended Visit Duration:** 2–4 hours
- **Best Time to Visit:** Weekday mornings to avoid school-group peaks; year-round (indoor)
- **How to Reach:**
  - **Nearest Metro Station:** Supreme Court (formerly Pragati Maidan, Blue Line), approx. 1–1.3 km / 15 minutes' walk or e-rickshaw
  - **Nearest Bus Stop:** Bhairon Marg/Pragati Maidan stops; DTC routes along Mathura Road (verify current route numbers locally)
  - **Other Options:** Autos on Bhairon Marg; paid parking near Pragati Maidan complex
- **Suitable For:** kids, families, students, budget travellers
- **Nearby Attractions:** Purana Qila (No. 20), National Crafts Museum (No. 13), National Zoological Park (No. 45)

### 13. National Crafts Museum & Hastkala Academy

- **Category:** Museum (secondary: cultural)
- **Tags:** handicrafts, textiles, artisans, village-complex, shopping, photography, cultural
- **Why It Is Famous:** The Crafts Museum on Bhairon Marg, developed from the 1950s under the vision of Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay and designed in its present campus form by architect Charles Correa, documents India's living craft traditions. Its collection of over 30,000 objects includes bronzes, terracotta, wood carving, tribal and folk art, and one of the country's finest textile galleries. A recreated village complex displays full-scale rural dwellings from different states, and resident artisans demonstrate and sell crafts in a monthly rotating programme. The museum shop and Café Lota (known for regional Indian cuisine) are destinations in their own right. It pairs naturally with Purana Qila and Pragati Maidan visits.
- **Timings:** Open Tuesday to Sunday, approx. 10:00 AM – 5:00 PM (galleries); artisan demonstration area and café may run longer; closed on Mondays. Verify locally.
- **Entry Fee:** Indian nationals approx. ₹20–₹30; foreign nationals approx. ₹200–₹300. Verify locally.
- **Recommended Visit Duration:** 1.5–2 hours
- **Best Time to Visit:** Late morning; October–March for the open-air village section
- **How to Reach:**
  - **Nearest Metro Station:** Supreme Court (Blue Line), approx. 900 m – 1.2 km / 12–15 minutes' walk
  - **Nearest Bus Stop:** Bhairon Mandir/Pragati Maidan stops on Bhairon Marg (verify current route numbers locally)
  - **Other Options:** Autos on Mathura Road/Bhairon Marg; limited parking on site
- **Suitable For:** art lovers, shoppers, foodies, families, solo travellers
- **Nearby Attractions:** Purana Qila (No. 20), National Science Centre (No. 12), National Zoological Park (No. 45)

### 14. Pradhanmantri Sangrahalaya (Prime Ministers' Museum) & Nehru Planetarium

- **Category:** Museum (secondary: family/entertainment)
- **Tags:** interactive, technology, history, kid-friendly, indoor, air-conditioned, educational
- **Why It Is Famous:** Located on the Teen Murti Estate, the former residence of India's first Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru, this campus combines the historic Teen Murti Bhavan museum with the Pradhanmantri Sangrahalaya, opened in 2022, which profiles every Indian Prime Minister since independence. The new wing uses immersive technology — holograms, kinetic sculptures, interactive screens and virtual-reality experiences such as a simulated helicopter ride — to present India's post-1947 political and developmental history. Nehru's preserved study, bedroom and library offer a period snapshot of mid-century official life. The same estate houses the Nehru Planetarium, a long-standing destination for sky shows aimed at students and families. The landscaped grounds include a 14th-century hunting lodge, Kushak Mahal.
- **Timings:** Museum open Tuesday to Sunday, approx. 10:00 AM – 6:00 PM; closed on Mondays. Planetarium show timings vary — verify locally.
- **Entry Fee:** Museum approx. ₹50–₹100 (Indian adults, online/offline rates differ); foreign nationals higher (approx. ₹750 for full experience tickets); planetarium shows ticketed separately (approx. ₹100–₹150). Verify locally.
- **Recommended Visit Duration:** 2–3 hours
- **Best Time to Visit:** Weekday mornings; year-round (largely indoor)
- **How to Reach:**
  - **Nearest Metro Station:** Lok Kalyan Marg (Yellow Line), approx. 1 km / 12 minutes' walk
  - **Nearest Bus Stop:** Teen Murti stop; DTC routes along Teen Murti Marg (verify current route numbers locally)
  - **Other Options:** Autos readily available; on-site parking
- **Suitable For:** families, students, history buffs, kids
- **Nearby Attractions:** Nehru Park (No. 42), National Rail Museum (No. 10), Rashtrapati Bhavan (No. 26)

### 15. Shankar's International Dolls Museum

- **Category:** Museum (secondary: family/entertainment)
- **Tags:** kid-friendly, dolls, indoor, budget, educational, air-conditioned, quick-visit
- **Why It Is Famous:** Founded in 1965 by political cartoonist K. Shankar Pillai, this museum on Bahadur Shah Zafar Marg holds one of the world's largest collections of costume dolls — over 6,500 exhibits from around 85 countries. The collection began with dolls Shankar received as gifts during international travel with Indian delegations and grew through worldwide contributions. Displays are arranged geographically, with a substantial Indian section showing regional bridal costumes and dance forms in miniature. An in-house workshop historically produced Indian costume dolls that were sent abroad as cultural gifts. It is a compact, inexpensive stop that pairs well with central-Delhi itineraries for families with young children.
- **Timings:** Open Tuesday to Sunday, approx. 10:00 AM – 5:30 PM (last entry approx. 5:00 PM); closed on Mondays. Verify locally.
- **Entry Fee:** Approx. ₹25 (adults), ₹10 (children). Verify locally.
- **Recommended Visit Duration:** 45 minutes – 1 hour
- **Best Time to Visit:** Any time; useful indoor option in summer and monsoon
- **How to Reach:**
  - **Nearest Metro Station:** ITO (Violet Line), approx. 400–600 m / 6–8 minutes' walk
  - **Nearest Bus Stop:** ITO crossing stops; numerous DTC routes on Bahadur Shah Zafar Marg (verify current route numbers locally)
  - **Other Options:** Autos plentiful at ITO; limited street parking
- **Suitable For:** kids, families, budget travellers
- **Nearby Attractions:** Agrasen ki Baoli (No. 23), Bengali Market (No. 37), Red Fort (No. 16)

## Section 3: Historical Places / Monuments

### 16. Red Fort (Lal Qila)

- **Category:** Historical (secondary: museum)
- **Tags:** unesco-heritage, mughal-architecture, heritage, photography, sound-and-light-show, old-delhi, iconic

### 17. Qutub Minar Complex

- **Category:** Historical
- **Tags:** unesco-heritage, heritage, photography, sultanate-architecture, iconic, south-delhi, couples
- **Why It Is Famous:** The Qutub Minar, begun by Qutb-ud-din Aibak around 1199 and completed by successors, is a 72.5-metre fluted sandstone-and-marble victory tower — the tallest brick minaret in the world — and a UNESCO World Heritage Site (inscribed 1993). The surrounding complex contains the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque (the earliest extant mosque in North India), the intricately carved Alai Darwaza gateway, the unfinished Alai Minar, and the 4th–5th-century Iron Pillar famed for its rust-resistant metallurgy. The minaret's five storeys, separated by projecting balconies and bands of Quranic calligraphy, document the evolution of early Indo-Islamic architecture. The complex anchors the Mehrauli heritage zone, Delhi's oldest continuously inhabited area. Evening illumination makes it a popular photography subject from surrounding parks.

### 18. Humayun's Tomb

- **Category:** Historical
- **Tags:** unesco-heritage, mughal-architecture, gardens, photography, romantic, heritage, wheelchair-accessible
- **Why It Is Famous:** Humayun's Tomb, commissioned around 1565–1572 by the emperor's widow Bega Begum and designed by Persian architect Mirak Mirza Ghiyas, is the first grand garden-tomb of the Indian subcontinent and a UNESCO World Heritage Site (inscribed 1993). Its red sandstone and white marble mausoleum, set in a Persian charbagh (four-quartered garden) with water channels, directly influenced the design of the Taj Mahal. The complex also contains Isa Khan's octagonal tomb-garden (pre-dating the main tomb), the Barber's Tomb, and restored gateways and pavilions. A major Aga Khan Trust for Culture restoration returned the gardens and finishes to their original character, and an interpretation centre/site museum has been developed at the entrance. ### 19. India Gate & Kartavya Path

### 20. Purana Qila (Old Fort)

### 21. Jantar Mantar

- **Category:** Historical (secondary: science heritage)
- **Tags:** astronomy, heritage, photography, central-delhi, quick-visit, educational, architecture
- **Why It Is Famous:** Jantar Mantar is an open-air astronomical observatory built in 1724 by Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II of Jaipur, the first of five such observatories he constructed across North India. Its 13 monumental masonry instruments include the Samrat Yantra (a giant equinoctial sundial), the Misra Yantra (unique to Delhi, able to determine noon in multiple cities), and instruments for tracking celestial coordinates and eclipses. The observatory reflects an 18th-century effort to refine astronomical tables through large fixed instruments rather than telescopes. The bold geometric red-and-white structures are also of architectural interest and photograph distinctively. ### 22. Safdarjung's Tomb

- **Category:** Historical
- **Tags:** mughal-architecture, heritage, quiet, photography, gardens, quick-visit, couples
- **Why It Is Famous:** Safdarjung's Tomb, completed in 1754 for the Nawab of Awadh and Mughal prime minister Safdarjung, is often described as the last major garden-tomb of the Mughal architectural tradition. Built in sandstone and marble within a charbagh garden, it displays the late-Mughal style — a tall bulbous dome, multi-chambered pavilions on the garden axes, and materials partly reused from the earlier tomb of Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khanan. Its slightly elongated proportions are frequently contrasted by historians with the earlier Humayun's Tomb, making the two a useful architectural comparison pair. The site is markedly less crowded than Delhi's headline monuments, offering calm lawns in the middle of Lutyens' Delhi. It sits directly opposite Lodhi Garden's southern side, enabling an easy combined visit.

### 23. Agrasen ki Baoli

- **Category:** Historical
- **Tags:** stepwell, free-entry, photography, quick-visit, central-delhi, offbeat, film-location
- **Why It Is Famous:** Agrasen ki Baoli is a 60-metre-long, 15-metre-wide historical stepwell on Hailey Road, hidden among the high-rises near Connaught Place. ### 24. Tughlaqabad Fort

- **Category:** Historical

### 25. Mehrauli Archaeological Park

### 39. Lodhi Garden

### 50. Okhla Bird Sanctuary

- **Category:** Family/Entertainment (secondary: park/nature)
- **Tags:** birdwatching, nature, photography, winter-visit, wetland, quiet, budget
- **Why It Is Famous:** Okhla Bird Sanctuary, a roughly 4 sq km protected wetland at the Okhla barrage on the Yamuna between Delhi and Noida, is one of the NCR's premier birdwatching sites and a designated Important Bird Area. Over 300 bird species have been recorded here, with winter bringing large flocks of migratory waterfowl — ducks, geese, waders and raptors — from Central Asia and beyond, alongside resident species such as painted storks, herons and purple swamphens. Watchtowers, walking trails along the bund and reed-fringed open water support both casual family visits and serious birding/photography. The sanctuary offers an accessible wildlife experience without leaving the metro network. Early winter mornings provide the richest sightings.
- **Timings:** Open daily, approx. 7:00 AM – 5:30 PM (winter) and 7:00 AM – 6:00 PM (summer). Verify locally.

### 52. Kalkaji Mandir

- **Category:** Religious
- **Tags:** spiritual, hindu-temple, ancient, free-entry, south-delhi
- **Why It Is Famous:** One of Delhi's oldest active Hindu temples, dedicated to the goddess Kalka (a form of Kali); the present structure's oldest portions date to around 1764, built under Maratha-era patronage, making it among South Delhi's principal Shaktipeeth-tradition shrines. The temple has a twelve-sided plan, each face corresponding to a zodiac sign, with the deity enshrined as a Pindi (aniconic stone form) flanked by tiger figures in red stone. ### 53. Moti Masjid (Red Fort)

- **Category:** Religious (secondary: historical, mughal-architecture)
- **Tags:** heritage, mughal-architecture, spiritual-history, photography, inside-red-fort
- **Why It Is Famous:** A small private mosque built in white marble inside the Red Fort complex by Mughal emperor Aurangzeb, completed in 1663 for his own use and that of the imperial ladies' quarters (Zenana Mahal). Named "Pearl Mosque" for its marble, it features a three-arched prayer hall with three bulbous domes (originally gilded copper) and a small marble ablution fountain in its courtyard. It is no longer used for regular worship and is visited as part of a Red Fort tour, not separately ticketed.
- **Timings:** Same as Red Fort's visiting hours, Tuesday–Sunday, dawn to dusk; closed Mondays. Verify locally.

### 54. Fatehpuri Masjid

- **Category:** Religious (secondary: historical)
- **Tags:** heritage, mughal-architecture, old-delhi, free-entry, chandni-chowk
- **Why It Is Famous:** Built in 1650 by Fatehpuri Begum, one of Emperor Shah Jahan's wives, standing at the western end of Chandni Chowk. It is the second-largest mosque in Old Delhi after Jama Masjid, built of red sandstone with a fluted dome, tall minarets and a vast prayer hall fronted by seven large arches. During the 1857 uprising it was used to station troops and was later auctioned off by the British to a local merchant before reverting to religious use.

### 55. Gurdwara Sis Ganj Sahib

- **Category:** Religious (secondary: historical)
- **Tags:** spiritual, free-entry, langar, sikh-heritage, old-delhi, chandni-chowk

### 56. Saint James' Anglican Church (Skinner's Church)

- **Category:** Religious (secondary: historical)
- **Tags:** heritage, colonial-architecture, free-entry, kashmere-gate, quiet
- **Why It Is Famous:** Delhi's oldest surviving church, commissioned by Colonel James Skinner (of Skinner's Horse) as a vow made while recovering from a battle wound, begun in 1826 and consecrated in 1836 after roughly a decade of construction. - **Timings:** Generally open mornings and evenings; hours vary around services and events. Verify locally.

### 57. Cathedral Church of the Redemption

### 58. Roshanara Bagh

### 59. Coronation Park

- **Category:** Park/Garden (secondary: historical)
